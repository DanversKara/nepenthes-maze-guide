STARTER CORPUS - REPLACE THIS FILE BEFORE REAL USE
Nepenthes trains its Markov babbler on this file at every startup.
This starter is only a few dozen lines so the container can boot and you
can verify the maze works. For real use replace corpus.txt with a large
plain-text file - around 60000 lines is the upstream example size - for
example a public-domain book from Project Gutenberg with the license
header stripped out. Keep it plain text, one sentence per line works
well. The larger and more varied the corpus, the less repetitive the
generated pages look to a crawler.
The pitcher plant waits beside the trail and offers a deep cup to any
traveler who wanders too close to the edge of the bog in summer.
A web crawler follows every link it finds because finding links is the
only job it was given and it has no way to judge what it has found.
The garden keeps records of rainfall and soil and the slow progress of
seedlings through the short northern spring and the long dry autumn.
Engineers measure the river at the bridge each morning and write the
height in a ledger that stretches back through a hundred flood years.
The library at the edge of town lends maps and repair manuals and old
newspapers bound in cloth to anyone who asks at the front desk.
A careful mechanic listens to an engine the way a doctor listens to a
chest and writes down every rattle before opening the housing at all.
The market opens before dawn and the bakers arrive first followed by
the farmers with crates of greens and jars of honey from the hills.
Sailors read the clouds over the harbor and decide whether the crossing
will be calm enough for the small boats to leave before the evening.
The archive holds letters written by surveyors who crossed the desert
with camels and brass instruments and returned with charts of wells.
Every network is a graph of promises between machines that agree to
answer when called and to wait politely when the line is busy.
The observatory dome turns slowly through the night following a star
that will not be visible again from this valley for another decade.
Cooks in the mountain lodge keep a pot of soup on the stove from the
first snow until the road opens again and the trucks return in May.
